# Question 15: What are the most common objections during sales cycles?

## Answer

The most common objection in SaaS sales is **"Not Right Now"** — timing and status quo objections account for 40-60% of lost deals ending in "no decision." Price objections, while frequent, are often proxies for unproven value. Only 23% of pricing objections are genuinely about budget constraints.

---

## Executive Summary

| Objection Category | Frequency | Root Cause |
|-------------------|-----------|------------|
| **Timing / Status Quo** | 40-60% of losses | Inertia, no urgency |
| **Price / Budget** | Very common | Unproven value, budget constraints |
| **Authority** | Common | Wrong stakeholder, need approval |
| **Competition** | Moderate | Incumbent loyalty, switching costs |
| **Trust / Risk** | Moderate | Implementation fear, vendor concerns |
| **Need / Value** | Moderate | Unclear problem, weak business case |

> "48% of B2B sales opportunities end with 'no decision' rather than a competitor win." — Gartner

## Objection Category 1: Timing / Status Quo

### Common Phrases

| Objection | What It Really Means |
|-----------|---------------------|
| "Not right now" | No urgency; other priorities |
| "Maybe next quarter" | Delaying decision |
| "We're too busy" | Implementation seems disruptive |
| "Let's revisit in 6 months" | Pushing off indefinitely |
| "It's not a priority" | Haven't connected to their pain |
| "We're happy with what we have" | Status quo bias |
| "If it ain't broke, don't fix it" | Change aversion |

### Why This Matters

> "40-60% of deals are lost because of no decision, according to The Jolt Effect. This makes no-decision deals extremely costly."

### Root Causes

| Cause | Implication |
|-------|-------------|
| **No compelling event** | Lack of deadline or trigger |
| **Hidden cost of inaction unknown** | They don't see the pain of staying |
| **Fear of change** | Implementation risk feels greater than benefit |
| **Competing priorities** | Other projects taking resources |
| **Budget cycle timing** | Not in this year's budget |

### Handling Strategies

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Quantify cost of inaction** | "What's the cost of each month without AI automation?" |
| **Create urgency** | Tie to compliance deadline, fiscal year, competitive pressure |
| **Start small** | "Let's do a 30-day pilot with one team" |
| **Find the trigger** | "What would need to happen for this to become urgent?" |
| **Time-limited incentive** | Premium onboarding, pricing lock, bonus features |

### Example Response

> "I understand timing is tight. Many of our customers felt the same way before they realized they were losing 20 hours per week on manual ticket routing. If we could show you that value in a 2-week pilot with zero commitment, would that change the equation?"

## Objection Category 2: Price / Budget

### Common Phrases

| Objection | What It Really Means |
|-----------|---------------------|
| "It's too expensive" | Haven't proven the value |
| "We don't have the budget" | Not prioritized in current budget |
| "Your competitor is cheaper" | Need to justify premium |
| "Can you do better on price?" | Standard negotiation |
| "We need to cut costs" | Economic pressure |
| "The ROI isn't clear" | Business case not made |

### Why This Matters

> "Only 23% of pricing objections are genuinely about budget constraints." — Corporate Visions

Most price objections are really **value objections in disguise**.

### Root Causes

| Cause | Implication |
|-------|-------------|
| **Value not established** | Features discussed, not outcomes |
| **Wrong economic buyer** | Talking to user, not budget holder |
| **Comparison to wrong alternative** | Comparing to free tools or manual processes |
| **No ROI quantified** | Can't justify to finance |
| **Total cost unknown** | Looking at license only, not TCO |

### Handling Strategies

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Quantify ROI** | "Customers typically see 3x ROI in first year" |
| **Compare to true alternative** | "vs. ServiceNow, we're 10x less" |
| **Break down pricing** | Monthly per-user feels smaller than annual total |
| **Show TCO** | Include implementation, training, maintenance |
| **Offer pilot** | "Start with 10 users, prove value, then expand" |
| **Flexible terms** | Monthly billing, delayed start, success-based |

### Example Response

> "I hear you on budget. Let me ask—what's the cost of your current tool plus the 20 hours/week your team spends on manual workarounds? Our customers typically find the total cost is actually lower, plus they get those hours back. Can I walk you through a quick ROI calculation?"

## Objection Category 3: Authority / Decision-Making

### Common Phrases

| Objection | What It Really Means |
|-----------|---------------------|
| "I need to talk to my boss" | Not the decision-maker |
| "I'm not authorized to decide" | Need higher approval |
| "We have a committee" | Multiple stakeholders involved |
| "Procurement handles this" | Gatekeeper in the way |
| "Let me run it by the team" | Needs internal consensus |
| "The CIO makes these decisions" | Need executive engagement |

### Root Causes

| Cause | Implication |
|-------|-------------|
| **Single-threaded** | Only talking to one contact |
| **Wrong entry point** | Started with user, not buyer |
| **Champion not enabled** | Contact can't sell internally |
| **Unknown stakeholders** | Haven't mapped buying committee |

### Handling Strategies

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Enable the champion** | Provide ROI deck, talking points, case studies |
| **Ask to join the meeting** | "Can I present to your team directly?" |
| **Multi-thread early** | Engage 3+ stakeholders from start |
| **Map the committee** | "Who else would be involved in this decision?" |
| **Provide role-specific content** | Technical docs for IT, ROI for finance |
| **Executive outreach** | CxO-to-CxO connection if available |

### Example Response

> "That makes sense—these decisions usually involve multiple people. To help you make the case, I can provide an executive summary for your CIO and a technical overview for your team. Would it be helpful if I joined that conversation to answer questions directly?"

## Objection Category 4: Competition / Incumbent

### Common Phrases

| Objection | What It Really Means |
|-----------|---------------------|
| "We already use [Competitor]" | Switching costs feel high |
| "We're happy with our current solution" | No perceived reason to change |
| "Your competitor has feature X" | Specific capability gap |
| "We're locked into a contract" | Timing/financial barrier |
| "We've invested too much to switch" | Sunk cost fallacy |
| "ServiceNow is the standard" | Brand preference / safe choice |

### Root Causes

| Cause | Implication |
|-------|-------------|
| **Switching costs** | Migration, training, integration work |
| **Sunk cost fallacy** | Prior investment feels wasted |
| **Risk aversion** | "Better the devil you know" |
| **Incumbent relationships** | Personal ties to current vendor |
| **Brand trust** | Enterprise preference for known brands |

### Handling Strategies

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Uncover pain with incumbent** | "What would you improve about your current tool?" |
| **Quantify hidden costs** | Maintenance burden, unused features, slow support |
| **Offer migration support** | Data migration, training, parallel run |
| **Start as secondary vendor** | "Add us for one use case, expand later" |
| **Time to contract renewal** | "When does your contract renew?" |
| **Competitive differentiation** | Focus on where you're stronger |

### Competitor-Specific Responses

| Competitor | SolarWinds Positioning |
|------------|----------------------|
| **ServiceNow** | "Same outcomes at 1/10th the cost and complexity" |
| **BMC Helix** | "Modern UX without the maintenance burden" |
| **Freshservice** | "Enterprise compliance (HIPAA) that Freshservice can't match" |
| **Jira SM** | "Purpose-built ITSM without Atlassian ecosystem dependency" |
| **ManageEngine** | "Modern interface with enterprise-grade support" |

### Example Response

> "I understand you're using ServiceNow. Many of our customers came from ServiceNow when they realized they were paying for enterprise complexity they didn't need. Can I ask—are you using all the features you're paying for? What would you change if you could?"

## Objection Category 5: Trust / Risk

### Common Phrases

| Objection | What It Really Means |
|-----------|---------------------|
| "I've never heard of your company" | Brand awareness gap |
| "How do I know this will work?" | Need proof points |
| "What if implementation fails?" | Risk aversion |
| "We've had bad experiences before" | Burned by prior vendor |
| "Is your company stable?" | Vendor viability concerns |
| "What about security?" | Compliance/data concerns |
| "This seems complicated" | Implementation fear |

### Root Causes

| Cause | Implication |
|-------|-------------|
| **Brand unfamiliarity** | Haven't established credibility |
| **Past failures** | Burned by prior ITSM projects |
| **Implementation fear** | 30% of implementations fail (Gartner) |
| **Security concerns** | Data protection, compliance |
| **Vendor stability** | Fear of acquisition, EOL |

### Handling Strategies

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Provide references** | Similar industry, size, use case |
| **Share case studies** | Quantified success stories |
| **Offer pilot/POC** | Low-risk trial before commitment |
| **Security documentation** | SOC 2, HIPAA, penetration test results |
| **Implementation guarantees** | Go-live timeline, success criteria |
| **Company stability proof** | Revenue growth, customer count, backing |

### SolarWinds Trust Builders

| Proof Point | Application |
|-------------|-------------|
| **HIPAA compliance** | Healthcare-ready, BAA available |
| **SOC 2 Type II** | Security certified |
| **5,100+ customers** | Established customer base |
| **SolarWinds brand** | Part of larger, known company |
| **Implementation support** | Guided onboarding, success team |

### Example Response

> "I completely understand that concern. We have over 5,100 customers using Service Desk, including [similar company]. We're SOC 2 certified and HIPAA-ready. Would it help if I connected you with a reference customer in your industry who can share their experience?"

## Objection Category 6: Need / Value

### Common Phrases

| Objection | What It Really Means |
|-----------|---------------------|
| "We don't need this" | Problem not recognized |
| "Our current process works fine" | Pain not acknowledged |
| "I don't see the value" | Benefits not connected to their goals |
| "This isn't a problem for us" | Haven't uncovered the pain |
| "We have bigger priorities" | Not positioned as strategic |

### Root Causes

| Cause | Implication |
|-------|-------------|
| **Poor discovery** | Didn't uncover real pain points |
| **Wrong messaging** | Features, not outcomes |
| **Wrong persona** | Talking to someone without the problem |
| **Hidden pain** | They're used to workarounds |

### Handling Strategies

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Better discovery** | Ask about pain, not features |
| **Quantify the problem** | "How many tickets per month? Resolution time?" |
| **Show industry benchmarks** | "Best-in-class resolve in X hours" |
| **Expose hidden costs** | Manual work, overtime, employee frustration |
| **Connect to their goals** | Tie ITSM to their stated priorities |

### Example Response

> "I hear you. Can I ask a few questions? How many tickets does your team handle per month? What's your average resolution time? And how much time goes into manual routing and status updates? Often we find there's a significant hidden cost that's just become 'normal.'"

## Objection Handling Frameworks

### Feel, Felt, Found

> "I understand how you **feel**. Other customers **felt** the same way. What they **found** was..."

Best for: Emotional objections, trust issues

### Acknowledge, Explore, Respond

1. **Acknowledge**: "I hear your concern about..."
2. **Explore**: "Can you tell me more about...?"
3. **Respond**: "Based on what you've shared..."

Best for: Complex objections needing deeper understanding

### Isolate, Validate, Solve

1. **Isolate**: "Is that the main concern, or are there others?"
2. **Validate**: "That's a fair concern..."
3. **Solve**: "Here's how we address that..."

Best for: Ensuring you address the real objection

## Objection Prevention

### Best Practices

| Practice | Impact |
|----------|--------|
| **Better qualification** | Don't pursue poor-fit prospects |
| **Multi-thread early** | Engage multiple stakeholders |
| **Strong discovery** | Uncover pain before pitching |
| **Quantify value** | Build business case from start |
| **Address concerns proactively** | Raise and resolve before they object |
| **Provide proof points** | References, case studies, data |

### Pre-Emptive Messaging

| Likely Objection | Pre-Emptive Statement |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Price | "Customers typically see 3x ROI in year one..." |
| Implementation | "We deploy in weeks, not months..." |
| Competition | "Unlike [competitor], we include..." |
| Trust | "Over 5,100 customers trust us, including..." |
