# Question 11: How do enterprises evaluate and purchase ITSM solutions today?

## Answer

The ITSM buying process has fundamentally shifted: **buyers complete 60-70% of their evaluation before engaging vendors**, committee sizes have grown to 10-11 stakeholders, and AI capabilities have become a critical evaluation criterion. The process is non-linear, heavily influenced by peer reviews, and increasingly self-directed.

---

## Executive Summary

| Factor | 2025 Reality |
|--------|--------------|
| **Buyer journey completion before vendor contact** | 60-70% |
| **Average buying committee size** | 10-11 stakeholders |
| **Vendors shortlisted for demos** | 2-3 (down from 3.2) |
| **Buyers who prefer rep-free experience** | 75% |
| **Deals won by vendors in initial consideration set** | 90% |
| **Enterprise deals with purchase regret** | 56% |

## The Modern ITSM Buying Journey

### Journey Stages

The B2B buying journey is **non-linear** — only 10% of buyers follow a traditional funnel. Most loop back through stages multiple times.

| Stage | Buyer Activities | Vendor Visibility |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|
| **1. Problem Identification** | Recognize pain points, build business case | None — internal |
| **2. Solution Exploration** | Research categories, read reviews, consume content | Limited — content marketing |
| **3. Requirements Building** | Define must-haves, create RFP criteria | Moderate — thought leadership |
| **4. Shortlisting** | Narrow to 2-3 vendors, request demos | High — sales engagement |
| **5. Evaluation** | POC/trials, reference calls, negotiations | High — active selling |
| **6. Selection & Procurement** | Final approval, contracting, security review | High — deal closing |

### Key Insight: Late Vendor Engagement

> "Buyers don't engage a sales development representative until they are 69% through their buying journey."

**Implication:** By the time buyers contact SolarWinds, they've already:
- Defined their requirements
- Researched competitors
- Formed initial opinions
- Created a shortlist (often only 2-3 vendors)

## How Buyers Research ITSM Solutions

### Research Behavior

| Behavior | Percentage |
|----------|------------|
| Begin with category search (Google, G2, TrustRadius) | 54% |
| Consume 3-7 pieces of content before contacting vendor | 62% |
| Shortlist products they've heard of before research | 86% |
| Initiate first contact (vs. outbound sales) | 81% |

### Information Sources Used

| Source | Role in Decision |
|--------|------------------|
| **Peer reviews (G2, Gartner Peer Insights, TrustRadius)** | Primary — 90% say social proof influences shortlist |
| **Analyst reports (Gartner, Forrester)** | Validation — used to justify decisions to stakeholders |
| **Vendor websites** | Feature verification, pricing (if available) |
| **Content (blogs, webinars, whitepapers)** | Education during solution exploration |
| **Peer recommendations** | Highly trusted — reference calls critical |
| **Free trials / POC** | Hands-on validation before commitment |

### Gartner Peer Insights Impact

Gartner Peer Insights has become a critical factor in ITSM selection:
- Voice of the Customer reports synthesize peer reviews
- "Customers' Choice" designation signals market validation
- Buyers compare products side-by-side on deployment, support, capabilities
- Reviews by similar industries/company sizes weighted heavily

## The RFP Process

### Typical RFP Structure

| Section | Contents |
|---------|----------|
| **Objectives** | Current state, desired future state |
| **Scope** | What's in/out of scope for implementation |
| **Quantities** | Users, devices, tickets/month, integrations needed |
| **Timeline** | Key milestones, go-live target |
| **Requirements** | Functional, technical, security, compliance |
| **Evaluation criteria** | Weighted scoring matrix |
| **Response format** | How vendors should structure proposals |

### RFP Best Practices

| Practice | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **3-5 vendors maximum** | More = overwhelming; fewer = limited negotiating power |
| **Weighted scoring matrix** | Objective comparison across vendors |
| **Distinguish must-haves from nice-to-haves** | Prevents feature bloat in evaluation |
| **Start with business outcomes** | Not features — what problems are you solving? |
| **Include scenario-based questions** | "How would your tool handle X situation?" |

### Common RFP Mistakes

| Mistake | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| Too many requirements | Vendors can't differentiate; all claim compliance |
| Feature-focused vs. outcome-focused | Misses what actually matters to users |
| No stakeholder alignment | Conflicting priorities emerge late in process |
| Insufficient POC time | Can't validate vendor claims |

## Proof of Concept (POC) / Trial Evaluation

### POC vs. Demo

| Type | Purpose | Duration |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Demo** | Showcase features (vendor-controlled) | 1-2 hours |
| **Trial** | Self-service exploration (limited) | 14-30 days |
| **POC** | Prove fit in your environment | 2-4 weeks |

### POC Best Practices

1. **Define success criteria upfront** — What does "success" look like?
2. **Select realistic use cases** — Incident triage, change approvals, not edge cases
3. **Test in real environment** — Not just sandbox; connect to actual systems
4. **Involve multiple stakeholders** — IT, end users, security
5. **Assess vendor responsiveness** — Support during POC predicts long-term partnership
6. **Use historical data** — Baseline current metrics to measure improvement

### What to Evaluate in POC

| Area | Questions |
|------|-----------|
| **Ease of use** | Can agents be productive within days? |
| **Configuration** | How much customization requires vendor help? |
| **Integration** | Does it connect to your tools as promised? |
| **Performance** | Any latency or scaling issues? |
| **Reporting** | Can you build the reports you need? |
| **Migration** | How hard is data migration from current tool? |

## Deal Cycles by Segment

### Typical ITSM Sales Cycles

| Segment | Typical Cycle | Characteristics |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| **SMB (<200 employees)** | 1-3 months | Single decision-maker or small team; quick decisions; price-sensitive |
| **Mid-Market (200-2,000)** | 3-6 months | Small committee (3-5); some customization; balanced evaluation |
| **Enterprise (2,000+)** | 6-18 months | Large committee (10-20); security reviews; procurement process; multiple approvals |

### What Extends Deal Cycles

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Committee size** | Each stakeholder adds complexity |
| **Security review** | Can add 4-8 weeks |
| **Procurement process** | Formal RFP, legal review, contracting |
| **Budget approval** | Fiscal year timing, budget availability |
| **Incumbent vendor** | Switching costs create inertia |
| **Integration requirements** | Custom integration needs slow decisions |

### What Shortens Deal Cycles

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Urgent pain point** | Compliance deadline, outage, growth |
| **Executive sponsor** | Top-down mandate accelerates decisions |
| **Pre-existing relationship** | Prior vendor familiarity reduces evaluation |
| **Transparent pricing** | No lengthy negotiation required |
| **Quick POC success** | Proof points accelerate approval |

## Buying Committee Dynamics

### Typical ITSM Buying Committee

| Role | Perspective | Influence |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **CIO / IT Director** | Strategic alignment, risk | Decision-maker |
| **IT Operations Manager** | Day-to-day usability, features | Heavy influencer |
| **Service Desk Manager** | Agent experience, efficiency | Heavy influencer |
| **Procurement** | Cost, compliance, contracting | Gatekeeper |
| **Finance / CFO** | Budget, ROI, TCO | Final approval (79% of purchases) |
| **Security** | Data protection, compliance | Veto power |
| **End Users** | Ease of use, self-service | Input |
| **IT Architects** | Integration, scalability | Technical validation |

### Committee Dynamics

| Dynamic | Data Point |
|---------|------------|
| Average stakeholders involved | 10-11 |
| Stakeholders NOT in IT | 2/3 (two-thirds) |
| Purchases with buyer remorse | 56% |
| Primary cause of remorse | Disagreement over objectives, vacillation |

### Navigating the Committee

| Strategy | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| **Map all stakeholders** | Identify decision-makers, influencers, blockers |
| **Understand each perspective** | CIO wants strategy; ops wants usability; finance wants ROI |
| **Build consensus** | Involve stakeholders early to prevent late objections |
| **Provide role-specific content** | Technical docs for architects; ROI calculators for finance |
| **Identify champion** | Find internal advocate who will sell internally |
